Set up SignLens beta

SignLens shows what a Solana transaction does before your wallet asks you to sign it. It holds no keys: your wallet still signs, and asks you as it always has.

First, let SignLens reach the API and your RPC

This browser has not let SignLens reach them yet, and without them nothing can be explained.

1. Your wallet addresses

SignLens calls only these addresses “you” in its explanations, so a site cannot pass off its own address as yours. Copy them from your wallet (Phantom, Solflare, Backpack, …).

An address you leave out is asked about the first time a site signs with it. You can change them any time in SignLens's settings.

2. Use it in a dapp

  1. In the dapp's list of wallets, choose SignLens rather than your wallet. SignLens connects through your wallet, which asks you as usual.
  2. Each request then opens SignLens's window first. Read what the transaction does, then continue to your wallet, or reject.

Dapps reconnect the wallet you used last. Where one connects your wallet directly, SignLens is skipped: disconnect it there, and choose SignLens.

Using more than one wallet? Choose which one SignLens passes requests to in its popup while you are on a dapp: it lists the wallets it finds there.

3. Pin SignLens to the toolbar

What leaves your browser

In this beta, each transaction you are asked to sign is sent to SignLens's API to be explained, with your addresses and your address book, so the explanation can say “you” and name your contacts. The server keeps verdicts and finding codes, not the transaction, unless you choose to send it with a rating. Lookup tables, accounts and a simulation are read from the RPC in the settings.

All of this, and more, is in the settings in SignLens's popup.
